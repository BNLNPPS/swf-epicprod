Pressure front

The front keeps every production queue supplied from the ready task queue, in priority order, against a set point in hours of work at the queue's capacity, and never feeds a queue whose gates are red. Mode {{ mode }}{% if mode == 'shadow' %} (decides and records, submits nothing){% endif %}; front {% if enabled %}on{% else %}off{% endif %}. {% if never_run %}No cycle has run yet.{% else %}Last cycle {{ cycle_at }} ({{ state.duration_s }} s), every five minutes.{% endif %} The switches and set points are the front settings on the System page; the numbers come from the queue census; the decisions are on the action stream; the plan is CONTINUOUS_PRODUCTION.md.

Definitions
  • State, what the front decided for the queue on its last cycle:
    • supplied: committed depth is at or above the low-water mark; nothing to do.
    • would feed (shadow mode) or fed: the next eligible task was released, or in shadow mode would have been.
    • awaiting observation: a feed is younger than the activation window (600 s); it counts as committed depth until the census shows its jobs.
    • idle capacity: work is pending but the queue runs below 30% of its ceiling, and has done so for longer than its start latency since the front's last feed; the worker supply or the site, not the front, is short.
    • degraded: a gate is red (canary, credential, breaker, or a failure detector); the reason names which.
    • oversize: the next eligible task alone exceeds the high-water mark; the operator decides.
    • held: the front or the queue's feed switch is off, a downtime is declared for the queue, a cap would be passed, the feed would pass the high-water mark, the task cannot be sized, or the queue has no calibration.
    • no work: nothing in ready is eligible for the queue.
    • error: the decision itself failed; the reason carries the error.
  • Reason, the code behind the state, in the order the cycle checks them:
    • front_off: the front is off (the front switch on the System page).
    • queue_off: this queue's feed is off (its feed switch).
    • breaker_open: this queue's breaker is open.
    • declared: CRIC declares a downtime for the queue, in force or starting within the high-water mark's hours; a hold that lifts itself when the window ends, never a breaker.
    • credential: the nightly production credential check is not ok, or older than 36 h.
    • canary: the site canary's verdict on the queue is failing, or its evidence is older than 24 h.
    • burn_through: in the last 6 h at least 10 of the queue's jobs failed, half of them fast, and none finished.
    • failure_window: in the last 6 h at least 50 of the queue's jobs ended and 60% of them failed.
    • awaiting_observation: the front fed the queue less than 600 s ago and waits for the census to show the jobs.
    • no_calibration: the queue finished no production job in 14 days, so its hours cannot be computed.
    • supplied: committed hours are at or above the low-water mark.
    • not_pulling: work is pending but the queue runs below 30% of its capacity, and has since before its start latency elapsed after the front's last feed.
    • no_eligible_task: no task in ready is pinned to the queue and passes the readiness checks.
    • unsized_task: the next task's job count cannot be read (no per-job event count or no matched input).
    • oversize_task: the next task alone exceeds the high-water mark.
    • would_exceed_high: committed hours plus the next task would pass the high-water mark.
    • task_cap: the feed would pass the task cap.
    • job_cap: the feed would pass the job cap.
    • would_feed:<task> or fed:<task>: the task the front released, or in shadow mode would have.
    • census_unavailable: the queue census could not be read this cycle; every queue holds.
    • decision_error: the decision for this queue failed; the record carries the error.
  • Job-hours: one slot busy for one hour; the unit of work, comparable across queues.
  • Capacity (h/h): job-hours per hour of clock, what the queue can run: its ceiling, the most jobs it ran at once in the last 14 days (or running now, if higher). Google at 5 slots supplies 5 h/h; OSG at 3,608 slots 3,608 h/h.
  • Hours (h) in the table: hours of work at that queue's capacity, the clock time the queue takes to run the work with every slot busy: job-hours divided by the queue's capacity.
  • Committed (h): the queue's not-yet-running work in hours at its capacity, plus the front's feeds still awaiting observation: not-started jobs times the median walltime of a finished job, divided by the ceiling. The quantity the set points are read against.
  • Runnable (h): the same as committed without the awaiting feeds: what PanDA holds for the queue now.
  • Not started (jobs): the queue's jobs PanDA holds but has not started (defined, waiting, assigned, activated, sent, starting).
  • Running (jobs): the queue's jobs running now.
  • Active tasks: production tasks pinned to the queue that have not ended; the task cap counts these.
  • Gates: the checks a queue must pass before the front feeds it: the canary, the credential, the breaker, and the two failure detectors (burn-through and the failure window). A red gate holds the queue.
  • Set points (h): two hours-of-work marks per queue. When the queue's committed hours fall below the low-water mark, the front feeds it; the front never feeds past the high-water mark. Set on the System page.
  • Caps: the most active tasks the queue may hold after a feed and, when set, the most not-started jobs; a feed that would pass either is held.
  • Ready: tasks in the ready state pinned to the queue, and how many of them pass the readiness checks.
  • Next candidate: the eligible task the front would release next, with its job count, its hours at capacity and its priority.
  • Canary: the site canary's verdict on the queue's health and the age of the evidence behind it. Values: healthy, degraded (failure rate at or above 30% over the canary's window), failing (at or above 60%), insufficient (fewer than 50 jobs in the window), unknown (no assessment), unread (the cycle could not read it; counts as red).
  • Credential: the nightly production credential check's outcome and age. Values: ok; anything else, none (no check on record) or unread, counts as red.
  • Last 6 h: the queue's production jobs finished and failed in the last six hours, and the failures that died fast (in under a quarter of the median walltime).
  • Breaker: a stop on feeding the queue that stays in place until someone clears it. Values: closed, feeding is allowed; open, feeding is stopped. It opens when a failure detector trips or an operator opens it; an operator closes it.
  • Feed: whether the front may feed this queue at all. Values: on, off; set by an operator. The front switch in the header turns the whole front on or off.
  • A feed is always recorded on the action stream; a hold when its reason changes, and hourly.

Supply and capacity

Ready work is what is queued in ready, in job-hours (one slot busy for one hour): each task's jobs times its queue's median walltime, by priority. Capacity is what the queues can run, in job-hours per hour of clock: each queue's ceiling, the most jobs it ran at once in the last 14 days, by queue.
